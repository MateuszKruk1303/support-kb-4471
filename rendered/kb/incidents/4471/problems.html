What each problem is

parser: the parser reads day-first dates month first

The shared parser reads every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a date with both parts at twelve or less comes out flipped: “7 April” goes out as “4 July”. This is the bug #4471 names, and the queued fix is for it. It reaches tenants through the app, by email, and through booking-v2’s form, whose hint (DD/MM/YYYY) matches how they write.

hint: booking-v3’s form tells people to type the month first

booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People do what the hint says and type the month first; the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first, and flips it. Only reports that came in through the form, at tenants on booking-v3, are this problem. The parser fix does not touch it: the hint has to match the tenant’s locale, or the form has to read what its hint asks for.

one-day: the date moves one day earlier

Not a flip. The date lands one day early, and only sometimes: “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”, one meeting reached Lisbon as Tuesday and Toronto as Monday, an invite arrived before the booking was made. That points at a date with no time being turned into a moment and shown in another time zone. The desk already read these reports as a one-day move (desk notes); here they are counted as their own problem instead of a footnote under parser.

How the split is made

  • One id, one report. The ticket export repeats some rows; each report counts once.
  • The tenant decides the cause, not intake. Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser; the cause comes from the tenant’s locale and booking form (causes.ts). The “tagged at intake as” column shows how far the two disagree. Do not count by tag.
  • The desk’s reading wins for one-day moves. A report in the desk’s one-day-move notes is that problem, whatever its tenant (problems.ts).
  • Every number here is built from the data at render time. When the desk merges more reports into #4471, the split follows. The tickets hold the counts as they were when they were filed.

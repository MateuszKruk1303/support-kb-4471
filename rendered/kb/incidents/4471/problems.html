What each problem is

parser: the parser reads day-first dates month first · fixed

The shared parser read every date month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a date with both parts at twelve or less came out flipped: “7 April” went out as “4 July”. This is the bug #4471 named, fixed by fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. It reached tenants through the app, by email, and through booking-v2’s form, whose hint (DD/MM/YYYY) matches how they write.

hint: booking-v3’s form told people to type the month first · won’t fix

booking-v3 printed MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People did what the hint said and typed the month first; the form read the date the tenant’s way, day first, and flipped it. Only reports that came in through the form, at tenants on booking-v3, are this problem. The parser fix did not touch it. It is won’t fix because booking-v3 is retired: with the form gone, nothing prints that hint.

one-day: the date moves one day earlier · open

Not a flip. The date lands one day early, and only sometimes: “Tuesday 6 October showed as Monday”, one meeting reached Lisbon as Tuesday and Toronto as Monday, an invite arrived before the booking was made. That points at a date with no time being turned into a moment and shown in another time zone. The desk already read these reports as a one-day move (desk notes), filed them under parser by tenant and footnoted them. A day-first parser does not move a date by a day, so the parser fix leaves them as they are. Next step: check how booking dates cross time zones between the booking and the invite.

How the split is made

  • One id, one report. The ticket export repeats some rows; each report counts once.
  • The tenant decides the cause, not intake. Intake tags a report from the reporter’s browser; the cause comes from the tenant’s locale and booking form (causes.ts). The “tagged at intake as” column shows how far the two disagree. Do not count by tag.
  • Counted the way the desk counts. Every report stays where the desk’s own table files it, by tenant. A report in the desk’s one-day-move notes is counted as a one-day move as well, so those reports sit in two rows (problems.ts).
  • Every number here is built from the data at render time. The states come from the engineering update that closed #4471; the counts come from the export.
